◆ Under the Hood

How three frontier models build one honest dispatch.

Every Perplexing Trades report is the output of a three-model council — Claude Opus, Gemini, and GPT-5.4 — working in parallel against live market data, verified news, and financial databases. They analyze, disagree, and synthesize. The result is a research brief that used to take a team of analysts several hours to produce.

01 · What this is

A structured investment brief, not a news feed.

This is a research report — not a market wrap, not a newsletter, not an aggregator. Three frontier AI models read the same live data independently, each with a specific analytical lens. Their outputs are cross-referenced, their disagreements are surfaced, and the final report synthesizes the consensus while preserving the divergence points.

The models are grounded in real-time verified data via Perplexity's search and finance infrastructure — not their training memory. Every number you see was current at the time of generation and is traceable to a source you can click and verify.

◆ Agreement & Divergence
Where they agreed:

The dominant macro catalyst, the clearest beneficiary, the pre-earnings acceleration plays, the structural semi bifurcation, the key support/resistance level. Consensus is what you see as the headline of each dispatch.

Where they disagreed:

GPT-5.4 is typically most cautious on bounce trades (value-trap risk). Claude pushes breakout patterns hardest. Gemini weights data freshness and intraday reversals heaviest. These tensions are features, not bugs — they prevent groupthink and surface the real risks in the narrative.

03 · The real-time layer

Training memory doesn't trade. Live data does.

Every AI model has a training cutoff. Ask one about this morning's close without a real-time data layer and it will hallucinate or decline. What makes this research current is Perplexity's live search and finance infrastructure, which acts as a trusted bridge between the models and the world as it exists right now.

Rather than inferring recent data from training memory, Perplexity retrieves and validates actual figures — live prices, Fed statements, breaking news, earnings releases — and delivers them to the models as structured, verified context. Think of it as giving three brilliant analysts simultaneous access to a Bloomberg terminal instead of asking them to recall things from memory.

This is why every key claim carries a numbered citation. The [N] marker is a contract — a verifiable source at that URL that substantiates the claim. If a specific number appears without a citation, it shouldn't be in the report, and the models are instructed to call that out themselves.

04 · Guardrails

Four permanent rules that never loosen.

Rule 01
Freshness mandate
No cached data ever
Every price in the narrative, Key Numbers, Sector Vibes, and voice summary is pulled from finance_quotes at the moment of session generation. A fresh timestamp is printed on every page. Post-session QA re-pulls and flags anything greater than 2% off live.
Rule 02
Play diversity
≥3 new tickers per session
At least three of the five Alpha Plays must be tickers not recommended in the previous Morning or Evening session. No ticker may appear as an Alpha Play for more than two consecutive Morning sessions. This kills narrative-gravity and forces fresh scanning.
Rule 03
Self-healing QA
Fix stale data within 15 min
A ticker-aware parser audits every published page after each session. Any stale Key Number (greater than 2%) or play entry (greater than 5%) triggers an automatic re-pull, HTML patch, redeploy, and re-verify loop — up to three attempts. Silent if clean, notifies if healed.
Rule 04
Privacy wall
Bloomberg test on public
The public dispatch must pass the Bloomberg test: would a Reuters or Financial Times editor run this headline without knowing anything about a portfolio? No personal framing, no position data, no cost basis, no P&L on the public side — ever.
